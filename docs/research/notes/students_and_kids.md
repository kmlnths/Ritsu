# Students and households with kids: 10 countries

Compact tables first, then notes. "Est." = my own arithmetic from the cited figures, not a published number.

## Q1: What share of adults are students (tertiary enrolment)?

### Takeaway
Gross tertiary enrolment ratio (GER: students of any age divided by the population of the 5-year age group after secondary school; can exceed 100%) runs from about 30-50% in India, Malaysia and the Philippines to 75-108% in the rich English-speaking countries and Singapore. As a share of ALL adults, tertiary students are small everywhere: roughly 3 to 8% (est.). A "Studies" tile would suit a minority of users, but a sizable one among 18-24s, who are likely over-represented among early app users.

### Inferences
- Students as share of all adults (est., using the counts above over approximate adult populations: India ~950m, US ~260m, UK ~53m, Philippines ~70m): India ~4.5%, US ~7%, UK ~5%, Philippines ~7%. Treat as rough; adult population figures here are my approximations, not sourced in this note. For richer countries the share of 18-24s in study is much higher (GER 75-100%), so a "Studies" tile matters most for a young audience.
- Australia's and Singapore's high GER partly reflects international students and older learners, not only local young adults.
- "Studies" covers more than degrees (school students 16-18, adult courses, exam prep), so the real pool of people who might tick it is wider than tertiary enrolment.

- No source found giving "students as share of adult population" directly for any of the 10 countries.

## Q2: What share of households have children under 18 (or dependent children)?

### Takeaway
Roughly a quarter to a third of households in the rich English-speaking countries have a child under 18 at home (est. from family data); in India, the Philippines and Malaysia it is very likely a majority, since a quarter to a third of the whole population is under 15-18 and households are large. Definitions vary a lot (dependent child, under 18, "children" of any age), so compare with care.

### Cited Findings
- US: 39% of family households in 2024 included the householder's own children under 18 (54% of families in 1974); about 64% of households were family households; 38.5 million one-person households = 29% of households — [US Census Bureau press release 2024](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/families-and-living-arrangements.html)
- UK: 28.6 million households, 19.7 million families in 2024; 42.3% of families contained one or more dependent children; dependent child = under 16, or 16 to 18 in full-time education — [ONS Families and households in the UK: 2024](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/families/bulletins/familiesandhouseholds/2024)
- Canada: 10.3 million census families in 2021, 6 million (58%) with children; almost 40% of just under 15 million households have children at home; couples with and without children each about 25% of households — [Environics Analytics summary of 2021 Census](https://environicsanalytics.com/en-ca/resources/blogs/ea-blog/2022/07/13/census-2021-families-households-income-and-all-new-data-on-veterans); [StatCan families topic page](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/rt-td/families-familles-eng.cfm) (secondary summary; "children" in StatCan census families has no age limit)
- Australia: couples with children 43.7% of families; family households 70.5% of households (2021) — [ABS Household and families: Census 2021](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/people-and-communities/household-and-families-census/latest-release)
- New Zealand 2023 Census household composition: couple with children 26.25%, one parent with children 8.23%, one parent with children and others 2.09% — [Figure.NZ, from Stats NZ](https://figure.nz/chart/xFY5HpCzEvxkP2MC); [Stats NZ 2023 Census household highlights](https://www.stats.govt.nz/information-releases/2023-census-household-family-and-extended-family-highlights/)
- Ireland: 1,279,951 families in 2022, up 5% since 2016; families without children 394,052 (so about 69% of families have children of any age, est.); pre-school and early-school family cycle types 18% of family units; average 1.34 children per family — [CSO Census 2022 Profile 3, Families](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp3/censusofpopulation2022profile3-householdsfamiliesandchildcare/families/); [CSO press statement](https://www.cso.ie/en/csolatestnews/pressreleases/2023pressreleases/pressstatementcensus2022resultsprofile3-householdsfamiliesandchildcare/)
- Singapore: married couple households with children were 47.6% of resident households in 2025, down from 50.4% in 2020 — [SingStat General Household Survey 2025](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/publication-resources/general-household-survey-2025) (figure seen in search summary of GHS 2025 coverage)
- Philippines: voting-age (18+) = 63.4% of household population in 2020, so ~36.6% under 18; 30.7% under 15 — [PSA 2020 Census age and sex distribution](https://psa.gov.ph/content/age-and-sex-distribution-philippine-population-2020-census-population-and-housing)
- Malaysia: 8.2 million households in 2020; average household size fell from 4.2 to 3.8; 0-14s = 24.0% of population — [DOSM key findings MyCensus 2020](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/key-findings-population-and-housing-census-of-malaysia-2020-administrative-district); [DOSM Children Statistics 2020](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/children-statistics-malaysia-2020)
- India: under-15 share of household population fell from 28.6% (NFHS-4, 2015-16) to 26.5% (NFHS-5, 2019-21); 636,699 households surveyed — [NFHS-5 India report](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/FR375/FR375.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rich English-speaking countries: about 25-35% of households have a child under 18 (US ~25% est., UK ~29% est. for dependent children, NZ and Canada ~37-40% but that counts adult children too, so the under-18 share is lower).
- Singapore's 47.6% counts adult children still at home (common there), so the under-18 share is lower.
- India, Philippines, Malaysia: with 25-37% of people under 15/18 and 4+ person households, the share of households with at least one child under 18 is very likely above 50%. No official household-level figure found.
- For Ritsu: a "Kids" tile would apply to roughly 1 in 4 users in the West and likely 1 in 2 in India and South-East Asia, among adults running a household. Worth a setup tile, not a default.

### Gaps
- No official "share of households with at least one child under 18" found for India, Philippines, Malaysia, Ireland, Australia; US and UK shares are my arithmetic from family figures. The US ACS profile (DP02, "households with one or more people under 18") would give a direct figure but the API needs a key and was not reached.
- Canada, NZ, Australia, Singapore figures count children of any age living at home; an under-18 cut was not found in the search budget.
