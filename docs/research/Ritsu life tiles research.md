# Ten tiles fit almost every adult

Ritsu should keep ten setup tiles, but not the same ten: **keep Health, My home, Money, Car or bike, Kids, Pets and Travel; rename Parents to Family; merge Studies with Work or business into "Work or studies"; and add one new tile, Faith.** Three tiles (Health, My home, Money) apply to nearly every adult in all ten countries, so every person who finishes setup will tick at least one tile that fits them. The other tiles are "some people" tiles whose reach swings hugely by country: a car or bike is in about 9 in 10 homes in Malaysia, Australia, New Zealand and the US, but pets are in only about 1 in 12 Indian homes while they are in 7 in 10 Australian ones. Faith is the biggest gap in the current list: daily prayer is a majority habit in India and the Philippines and close to half of Americans do it, and nothing in the current ten covers it. Most things inside each tile repeat on the person's own date (their insurance, their MOT, their rent agreement), not a national date, which fits Ritsu's plan to ask "when is yours due?" one question a day. The main weakness of this report is missing numbers: several country figures come from industry surveys or my own rough arithmetic, and for some tile and country pairs there is no number at all. Those holes are marked plainly below, and none of them changes the recommendation.

## Three tiles fit everyone; the rest depend on the country

The simplest way to read this section: a tile is worth showing if a big share of adults would tick it. "Household" below means one home, which can hold several adults. Most official numbers are for all ages or all homes, not for the 18 to 55 year olds who are most likely to use Ritsu, so treat every figure as "roughly".

**My home, Money and Health apply to almost everyone.** Everyone lives somewhere, pays some bills, and has a body that needs the odd check-up. What changes by country is the flavour. About a third of homes rent in England, Canada, Australia and New Zealand ([English Housing Survey](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6746f3242cdbaeed4c527f5f/Annex_A_-_2023-24_EHS_Headline_Report_on_household_demographics_and_resilience.pdf), [ABS Census 2021](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/AUS), [Stats NZ](https://www.stats.govt.nz/news/home-ownership-increases-and-housing-quality-improves/)), while **Singapore is 91% owners** ([SingStat GHS 2025](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/publication-resources/general-household-survey-2025-households-and-housing)) and only about 11% of Filipino homes rent ([PSA 2020 Census](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/population-and-housing/node/1684059631)). For Health, the strongest numbers are about long-term conditions: **about 1 in 3 adults has high blood pressure in India (35.5%), Singapore (33.8%) and Malaysia (29.2%)**, and diabetes affects 9% to 16% of adults in those three countries ([Lancet, ICMR-INDIAB](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext), [Singapore MOH](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/national-population-health-survey-2024-shows-singaporeans-are-adopting-healthier-lifestyles---but-rising-obesity-is-a-concern/), [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-08311-9)). In the US, two thirds of adults saw a dentist in the past year ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/fastats/dental.htm)).

What this means in plain words. **Car or bike is a "most people" tile nearly everywhere except Singapore and the Philippines**, and the word "bike" is what makes it work in India, where two-wheelers are in over 60% of homes but cars in fewer than 1 in 10. **Kids fits about 1 in 4 homes in the West and probably half or more in India and South-East Asia**; the Asian figures are my estimates from how young the population is, not a direct count. **Parents is hard to measure** because countries define "carer" very differently: England's census says 9%, while a US survey with a looser meaning says 25%. The everyday version Ritsu cares about (booking a parent's check-up, paying their bill) is almost certainly bigger than any census number, and in India and Singapore most older people live with their grown-up children. **Pets is the most uneven tile**: a big tile in Australia, New Zealand and the Philippines, a small one in India. **Studies is the smallest tile, at roughly 4% to 7% of all adults**, though it matters a lot for 18 to 24 year olds. **Work or business is large in India and the Philippines** because so many people work for themselves. **Travel is patchy**: most British and Australian adults hold a passport, but my rough estimate for India is under 1 in 10 nationally (higher among city smartphone users, but that is unmeasured).

## Most tasks repeat on your own date, not a national one

Across all ten countries, the pattern is the same: **most repeating jobs run on a personal date** (the day your insurance started, your car's age, your birthday), and only tax deadlines and a few government windows are the same date for everybody. That strongly supports Ritsu asking "when is yours due?" instead of guessing. Rules also change often: New Zealand's car safety check moves to longer gaps from 1 November 2026 ([NZTA](https://vehicleinspection.nzta.govt.nz/news/changes-to-light-vehicle-inspections-from-1-november-2026)), Texas dropped most car safety inspections in 2025 ([Insurify](https://insurify.com/car-insurance/vehicle/state-vehicle-inspection/)), and both the Philippines and Malaysia pushed their 2026 tax deadlines back to 15 May ([Taxumo](https://www.taxumo.com/blog/bir-extends-2025-annual-income-tax-filing-deadline-to-may-15-2026/), [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/04/29/2025-tax-return-form-deadline-extended-to-may-15)). So Ritsu should store a normal date and let people change it, never call anything "overdue".

Three product lessons fall out of this table. First, **tax filing is only a real personal job for some people.** India had about 7.3 crore returns filed against roughly 95 crore adults, so fewer than 1 in 10 file ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/over-60-million-income-tax-returns-filed-for-2025-26-so-far-i-t-dept-125091300339_1.html)), and in the UK and New Zealand most employees never file. The Money tile should lead with bills and renewals and keep tax as a single optional item. Second, **jobs an employer already does should never appear** (Philippine SSS and PhilHealth for employees, India TDS, Singapore CPF). Third, **health items must be starting points only.** Screening gaps differ by country and changed recently (England moved cervical screening to every 5 years in July 2025), so Ritsu should say "every 2 years is common, ask your doctor for yours", never a rule.

A note on weak spots: the Asia task list rests on search summaries rather than full official pages, several Western items were "known rules" not re-checked this session, and Kids, Parents, Studies and Pets tasks were barely researched outside a few countries. The frequencies above are sensible defaults to ask about, not facts to show as law.

## Faith, family dates and long-term conditions are the big gaps

Some regular responsibilities fit none of the ten tiles. Here is how common each one is, as far as the notes show.

**Faith is the largest and clearest gap.** A 2025 Pew survey found **79% of Filipinos, 71% of Indians and 44% of Americans pray daily**, against 21% in the UK ([Pew 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/06/spiritual-and-religious-practices/)). An earlier, separate Pew India survey put daily prayer at 60% and found 81% of Indians have a home shrine ([Pew India 2021](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/06/29/religious-practices-2/)), so the honest Indian figure is "60% to 71%". Malaysian Muslims pay zakat fitrah every Ramadan and can claim a full tax rebate on income zakat paid by 31 December ([RinggitPlus](https://ringgitplus.com/en/blog/banking/everything-you-need-to-know-about-zakat-in-malaysia.html)). No figures were found for Malaysia, Singapore, Australia, New Zealand, Canada or Ireland prayer rates, or for fasting anywhere. Faith has its own rhythm (daily prayer, weekly worship, yearly fasts and festivals on moving lunar dates) that does not sit well inside Health or Family.

**Long-term conditions are very common but belong in Health.** With about 1 in 3 adults having high blood pressure in India, Singapore and Malaysia, "check my BP" or "diabetes review" are everyday jobs. They only need an opt-in line inside Health that says "check and note it", with no target numbers and "ask your doctor how often". Figures for the Western countries were not gathered.

**Women's health is common and also belongs in Health.** A review of studies puts **period-app use at about 61% of women** ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13477691/), from the summary only), and a US survey of younger women found 62% ([JMIR 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e53146)). Screening letters (cervical, breast) also sit here. Ritsu should offer a gentle "my cycle" or "screening" item inside Health, not a separate tile.

**Domestic workers matter in parts of Asia only.** Singapore had **301,600 live-in domestic workers** at the end of 2024 ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/foreign-workforce-numbers)), which means a large share of Singapore homes; no reliable counts were found for India or Malaysia. Salary day, day off and permit renewal fit inside My home, shown only in Singapore, Malaysia, the Philippines and India.

**Birthdays, anniversaries and festivals are close to universal but unmeasured.** Nobody publishes "share of adults who buy festival gifts", so there are no numbers here. They fit naturally with parents under a wider "Family" tile. Festival dates in India and Malaysia move each year with the moon, so they need a calendar source before Ritsu can pre-fill them.

**Everything else is small or hobby-shaped:** plants, gym renewals, therapy, volunteering, phone backups and passwords. No country numbers were found for any of them. Document expiry (licence, passport, India's Aadhaar and PAN) applies to nearly everyone but happens only every few years; it fits as a "papers" item inside Money.

## Ten tiles is still the right size, with three changes

The research on "too many choices" does not say fewer is always better. A large 2010 review of 50 experiments found the average effect of more choice was close to zero ([Journal of Consumer Research](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/37/3/409/1827647)), but a 2015 review showed choice does overwhelm people when they are unsure what they want and want little effort ([Chernev and colleagues](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)), which is exactly how someone feels on a first setup screen. So ten plain, familiar words with pictures, where you can tick several or skip, is fine; twelve or fourteen would start to hurt. A Headspace test also found that **a short "what matters to you" step doubled first-session starts, from 31% to 63%, even though it did not change long-term use** ([Purchasely](https://www.purchasely.com/blog/headspace-behavioral-science-onboarding-experiment)). The tile screen earns its place, but it will not keep people on its own. No comparable app asks for life-admin dates at setup; bill and medicine apps set up one reminder at a time when the person adds a real item ([Paytm](https://paytm.com/support/bill-payment/setting-up-reminders-for-pending-bill-payments-on-paytm), [Medisafe](https://medisafe.com/solutions/for-patients)), which backs Ritsu's "one question a day" plan. Studies of phone reminders also show too many lead to people switching them off ([ResearchGate, mHealth notifications](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380521578_Investigating_Contextual_Notifications_to_Drive_Self-Monitoring_in_mHealth_Apps_for_Weight_Maintenance)), so each tile should seed only a few things, mostly monthly or yearly.

To stay at ten while adding Faith, one tile has to go. **Studies is the weakest tile on its own** (about 4% to 7% of adults) and overlaps with Work in how people think about it ("what I do all day"). Merging them into **"Work or studies"** keeps both groups covered. **Parents becomes "Family"**, which keeps parents at the centre but also holds birthdays, anniversaries and festivals, the biggest unmeasured gap. **Kids stays separate** because it is large (a quarter of Western homes, likely half in Asia) and has its own jobs (vaccines, school terms).

Show the everyone-tiles first, then the some-people tiles, as the onboarding notes suggest.

## Conclusion

The ten tiles already fit nearly every adult, because Health, My home and Money apply to everyone; the real question was never coverage but fit. The evidence says the list was built with a Western home in mind: Studies earned a whole tile while daily prayer, which matters to far more people in India and the Philippines than studying does, had nowhere to go. Swapping Studies for Faith (by merging Studies into Work) and widening Parents into Family makes the same ten tiles fit people in Manila and Mumbai as well as in Manchester, without making setup any longer.

The bigger lesson for Ritsu is that the tile screen is a menu, not a schedule. Because almost every date is personal and many rules shift each year, the tile's job is only to decide which "one question a day" to ask next. Since there are no real users to test with, the cheapest next step is a quiet count once the app is live: which tiles people tick in each country. If Faith or Family is rarely picked, or Travel is ticked by almost nobody in India, the data will say so within weeks, and a tile can be swapped without anyone noticing. Until then, ship the ten above.
