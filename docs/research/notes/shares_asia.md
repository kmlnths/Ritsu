# Life tile shares: India, Singapore, Philippines, Malaysia

Scope note: about 25 searches and fetches. Official figures are marked [OFFICIAL]. Industry, poll or market figures are marked [NON-OFFICIAL]. Anything I remember but could not confirm with a source this session is in Gaps, marked "unverified". "HH" means per household, "adult" means per person. Almost no source splits results for "urban, smartphone-owning, 18 to 55", so urban figures are used where they exist.

## Vehicle ownership (tile: Car or bike)

### Takeaway
Bikes fit India and Malaysia well and cars fit Malaysia. The tile is weaker in the Philippines, where most households have no car, and in Singapore, where about two thirds of households have no car. Showing "Car or bike" as one tile covers India (about 60%+ of households have a two-wheeler) and Malaysia (about 90% have a car).

### Cited Findings
| Country | Figure | Unit | Year | Source type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| India | Cars: about 8% of households nationally; about 14% urban, 4% rural | HH | NFHS-5 (2019-21) | [OFFICIAL] |
| India | Two-wheelers: "more than 60%" of households; cars "fewer than 10%"; urban cars about 14% (about 20 million HH) | HH | HCES 2023-24 (MoSPI) as summarised by Data For India | [OFFICIAL, via secondary summary] |
| Singapore | Car ownership 36.3% of households (up from 35.3% in 2017/18) | HH | Household Expenditure Survey 2023 | [OFFICIAL, via secondary summary] |
| Malaysia | 89.9% of households own a car; 75.4% own a motorcycle | HH | Basic Amenities Survey 2022 (DOSM) | [OFFICIAL] |
| Philippines | 25.2% of households own a motorcycle or tricycle; 5.9% own a car, jeep or van | HH | 2018 (PSA-based, via Statista) | [OFFICIAL, via aggregator] |
| Philippines (one region) | MIMAROPA: 48.0% of households own a motorcycle, scooter or tricycle | HH | 2020 Census | [OFFICIAL] |

- India NFHS-5 car share (8% nationally, about 14% urban vs 4% rural): [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/only-8-indian-families-own-cars-nfhs-finds-over-50-still-use-bicycles-bikes-scooters/971413/)
- India HCES 2023-24 vehicle shares: [Data For India](https://www.dataforindia.com/vehicle-ownership/)

- Malaysia 89.9% car, 75.4% motorcycle: [DOSM Basic Amenities Survey 2022](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/basic-amenities-survey-report-malaysia-2022). A 2024 edition exists: [DOSM 2024](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/basic-amenities-survey-report-malaysia-2024)

### Inferences
- Among urban Indian smartphone users, two-wheeler ownership is probably well above 60%, but I found no urban-only official two-wheeler figure. Treat this as an estimate.
- Malaysia's 89.9% is surprisingly high. It likely counts any car in the household, not per adult. It still means nearly every Malaysian user needs road tax and insurance reminders.

### Gaps
- No urban two-wheeler figure for India (the HCES tables have it; I did not open them).
- No national 2020 Census vehicle figure for the Philippines.
- No Singapore GHS 2025 car figure. The Household Expenditure Survey 2023 is the latest one found.

## Housing: renting vs owning, apartments (tile: My home)

### Takeaway
The tile applies to nearly everyone, owner or renter. Singapore is an owner nation (91% of households own their home, most in HDB flats). India's urban renting is about 28% (Census 2011, now old). Malaysia is about 77% owners and 20% renters. The Philippines is 57% owners, 11% renters, plus many informal or rent-free arrangements.

### Cited Findings
| Country | Figure | Unit | Year | Source type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| India | 28% of urban households rent (21.7 million) | HH | Census 2011 | [OFFICIAL, old] |
| India | Rental share of urban housing fell from 37% (1991) to 28% (2011) | HH | Census | [OFFICIAL] |
| India | CPR cites NSS 2019 showing a smaller rented share (about 15%, different method) | HH | NSS 2018-19 | [OFFICIAL, via think tank; method unclear] |
| Singapore | 91.2% of resident households own their home (87.9% in 2020) | HH | GHS 2025 | [OFFICIAL] |
| Malaysia | 76.9% of dwellings owner-occupied; 19.8% rented | HH | HIES/Basic Amenities 2019 | [OFFICIAL] |
| Philippines | 57.3% own or have owner-like possession of house and lot; 10.8% rent; 6.6% rent-free with consent | HH | 2020 Census | [OFFICIAL] |

- India Census 2011 rent figures: [ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/research/urban-rental-housing-in-india-towards-housing-for-all); dataset: [data.gov.in](https://www.data.gov.in/resource/stateut-wise-percentage-urban-households-living-rental-houses-census-india-2011-ministry); NSS 2019 note: [CPR](https://cprindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/RentalHousing-overview-India-Shubhagato.pdf)
- Singapore 91.2%: [SingStat GHS 2025](https://www.singstat.gov.sg/publication-resources/general-household-survey-2025-households-and-housing), as summarised by [Dollars and Sense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/interesting-financial-trends-we-spotted-in-singapores-general-household-survey-2025/)
- Malaysia 2019: [DOSM HIES & Basic Amenities 2019](https://v1.dosm.gov.my/v1/index.php?r=column/cthemeByCat&cat=120&bul_id=TU00TmRhQ1N5TUxHVWN0T2VjbXJYZz09&menu_id=amVoWU54UTl0a21NWmdhMjFMMWcyZz09)
- Philippines 2020 Census: [PSA](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/population-and-housing/node/1684059631)

### Inferences
- In India the "society maintenance fee" item is relevant only to apartment or gated-society dwellers. That is a minority nationally but likely large among metro smartphone users (estimate, no source).
- Singapore: an HDB-specific wording (town council fees, HDB loan) would fit most users.

### Gaps
- No official India figure for the share living in apartments or housing societies. Census 2011 house-type tables exist but were not found this session. The Census 2027 is not yet published.
- Unverified: Singapore GHS usually shows about 77% to 78% of resident households live in HDB flats. Confirm in the GHS 2025 tables.

## Children, household size, joint families (tile: Kids)

### Takeaway
Couples with children are the most common household type in Singapore (47.6% of resident households). Average household size is 3.8 (Malaysia) and 4.1 (Philippines), which implies children in a large share of households. India's detailed figure was not confirmed this session.

### Cited Findings
- Singapore: married couple-based households with children fell from 50.4% to 47.6% of resident households (HH, 2025) [OFFICIAL]: [MSF Family Trends Report 2025](https://www.msf.gov.sg/docs/default-source/research-data/family-trends-report-2025.pdf). Note: "children" here is not limited to under-18s.
- Malaysia: average household size 3.8 (was 4.2 in 2010); Kelantan 4.8, Sabah 4.7 (HH, Census 2020) [OFFICIAL]: [DOSM Census 2020 key findings](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/key-findings-population-and-housing-census-of-malaysia-2020-administrative-district)
- Philippines: average household size 4.1 (4.4 in 2015) (HH, Census 2020) [OFFICIAL]: [PSA](https://psa.gov.ph/content/household-population-number-households-and-average-household-size-philippines-2020-census)

### Inferences
- For users aged 18 to 55, "Kids" is probably relevant to between one third and one half, higher in the Philippines and India. This is an estimate, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No sourced share of households with a child under 18 for any of the four countries.
- Unverified: NFHS-5 average household size in India is about 4.4. Joint or extended households in NFHS are not confirmed.
- No joint or multigenerational household share found for Malaysia or the Philippines.

## Supporting or caring for ageing parents (tile: Parents)

### Takeaway
Most older people in all the countries checked live with a spouse or child: over 80% in India and about 80% in Singapore. The tile is therefore relevant to many adults, but the figures describe older people, not the share of 18 to 55 adults who support a parent.

### Cited Findings
- India: over 80% of older adults live with children or extended kin; 43.5% live with spouse and children, 26.7% with children and others (LASI 2017-18, per older adult) [OFFICIAL survey, via peer-reviewed paper]: [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9921119/), [PMC LASI transfers](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10737839/)
- India: 13.1% of older adults received money from children, three times the 4.4% who gave money to children (LASI 2017-18): [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10737839/)
- Singapore: about 80% of residents aged 65+ live with a spouse and/or child. GHS 2025 counts: 272,100 with spouse and at least one child; 222,800 with spouse only; 119,900 with children, no spouse (per older adult, 2025) [OFFICIAL]: [MSF Family Trends Report 2025](https://www.msf.gov.sg/docs/default-source/research-data/family-trends-report-2025.pdf), [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_45acf574880ac493ec638f72338e62d5/view)

### Inferences
- In India, co-residence with parents plus money flowing to parents suggests the tile is relevant to a majority of Indian adults with living parents. This is an estimate.

### Gaps
- No figure for the share of working-age adults who give money to or care for parents in any country.
- No figures found for the Philippines or Malaysia.

## Pets (tile: Pets)

### Takeaway
Pets vary the most across the four countries. The tile fits well in the Philippines (about 48% to 64% of households) and Malaysia (about half of people, mostly cats). It is a small tile in India (about 8% of households have a dog) and moderate in Singapore. None of these figures are official.

### Cited Findings

|---|---|---|---|---|
| India | Dog-owning households 7.1% (2020) to 8.2% (2025); cats 0.5% to 0.6% | HH | 2025 | [NON-OFFICIAL, market data] |
| India | Dog households 4.5% (2014) to 7.9% (2023) | HH | 2023 | [NON-OFFICIAL, Statista] |
| Singapore | About 34% of adults own a pet (dogs about 13%, cats about 12%) | adult | recent | [NON-OFFICIAL] |
| Singapore | Dog households 5.3%, cat 2.8% | HH | 2020 | [NON-OFFICIAL, market data] |
| Malaysia | 51.1% have at least one pet; of owners, 77.7% have cats, 14.9% dogs | adult | recent | [NON-OFFICIAL, consumer survey] |

- India: [Agriculture Canada sector report](https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/international-trade/reports-and-guides/sector-trend-analysis-pet-food-trends-india), [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1061350/india-households-share-with-pet-dogs-and-cats/)

- The two Singapore figures conflict: 34% of adults vs about 8% of households in 2020. Online-panel surveys usually overstate pet ownership. Treat Singapore as roughly 10% to 30%.

### Gaps

## Work or business: self-employment and tax filing (tiles: Work or business, Money)

### Takeaway
Self-employment is large in India (58% of workers overall; about 40% of urban workers) and the Philippines (about 28% own-account). It is moderate in Malaysia (about 15% to 19%) and not confirmed for Singapore. India's income tax returns (about 7 crore) cover only a small minority of adults.

- Philippines: [PSA LFS April 2025](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/labor-force-survey/node/1684076355)

### Inferences
- India: about 7 crore returns among roughly 95 crore or more adults means under 10% of adults file. The share is far higher among urban salaried smartphone users (estimate). The "Money" tile should lead with bills and subscriptions, not taxes, for India and the Philippines.
- Malaysia: the two figures conflict. 15.4% is the annual share reported by HR Online. The monthly count (about 3.1 million out of about 16.5 million employed) works out to about 19%. The gap may be definitional. Use about 15% to 19%.

### Gaps
- Singapore own-account worker share. The MOM "Labour Force in Singapore 2025" report has it, but it was not extracted: [MOM](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_PdfLibrary/mrsd_2025Labourforce.pdf). Unverified: roughly 8% to 10% of employed residents.
- No figures on tax filers as a share of adults for Singapore, Malaysia or the Philippines.
- India PLFS calendar 2025 self-employment figures were not extracted.

## Travel: passports (tile: Travel)

### Takeaway
India issues about 1.4 crore passports a year. The stock of valid passports, and the share of Indians holding one, was not confirmed. The share is still a small minority nationally. No figures were found for the other three countries.

### Cited Findings
- India: 1.39 crore passports issued in 2025 (1.5 crore passports and related services); 88.87 lakh e-passports (per year, 2025) [OFFICIAL, via news]: [Organiser](https://organiser.org/2026/06/24/359627/bharat/passport-seva-divas-india-issues-1-39-crore-passports-in-2025-as-passport-seva-network-expands-to-545-centres/), [PolicyEdge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/india-issued-nearly-89-lakh-e-passports-in-2025-as-overseas-worker-services-went-digital)
- India: 138 lakh passports issued in FY2025-26, up from 83 lakh a decade earlier: [NRI Affairs](https://www.nriaffairs.com/india-passport-boom-numbers-nri-2026/) [NON-OFFICIAL summary]

### Inferences
- Passports last 10 years. At about 1.3 to 1.4 crore a year, the valid stock is very roughly 10 to 13 crore, or about 7% to 9% of the population. This is my estimate, not an official figure. It will be much higher among urban smartphone users.

### Gaps
- No official stock of valid passports for India. No passport or outbound-travel shares for Singapore (likely very high), Malaysia or the Philippines.

## Smartphone ownership (sizing the user base)

### Takeaway
Smartphone ownership is very high in urban India. Among urban mobile owners aged 15 and over, 89.4% of men and 86.2% of women have a smartphone, and 97.6% of urban 15 to 29 year olds use a mobile phone (NSS CMS 2025). No figures were gathered for the other three countries this session.

### Cited Findings
- India: 89.4% of urban men and 86.2% of urban women aged 15+ who own a mobile phone own a smartphone; rural 79.2% and 75.6% (per adult, January to March 2025, NSS 80th round CMS: Telecom) [OFFICIAL]: [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2132330&reg=48&lang=2), [MoSPI PDF](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/may/doc2025529560001.pdf)
- India: 51% of rural women do not own a mobile phone (2025): [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/digital-india-divide-nso-rural-women-mobile-phone-ownership-gap-125052901804_1.html)

### Inferences
- In urban India, likely users are young, on smartphones and in cities. They are more often salaried, two-wheeler owning, renting, and living with parents than the national averages suggest.

### Gaps
- No smartphone ownership figures gathered for Singapore (IMDA), Malaysia (MCMC Internet Users Survey) or the Philippines (PSA / DICT NICTHS). Those are the sources to check next.
