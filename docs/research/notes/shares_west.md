# Life tile reach in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, NZ and Ireland (official stats, 2019 to 2026)

Scope note: this was a quick pass of about 17 searches covering six countries and ten tiles. Numbers marked **[official]** come from a national statistics office, census or government body. **[survey]** means industry or charity survey. **[weak]** means an aggregator or marketing site: treat it as a rough signal only. **[calc]** means I worked it out from cited numbers. Where nothing solid turned up, the table says "no good data found". Age 18 to 55 splits were almost never in the search snippets, so nearly every figure is national (all ages or all households).

## Car or bike: households with a car or van; motorbike ownership

### Takeaway
In every country with data, 78% to 94% of households have at least one car, so this tile applies to most users; the UK (England) is the lowest at 78%.

### Cited Findings
- US: 8.4% of households (about 11.1 million) had no vehicle available, ACS 2023 (household basis) [official, via aggregator summary] — [SparkMap](https://sparkmap.org/data-info/households-with-no-motor-vehicle/)
- US: about 1 in 10 Americans rarely or never drive (2024, adults) [survey] — [Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2024/11/14/1-in-10-americans-rarely-or-never-drive-a-car/)
- England: 22% of households had no car or van in 2024; 78% had at least one (National Travel Survey 2024, households) [official] — [DfT NTS 2024 factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2024/nts-2024-factsheet)
- Australia: 7.3% of occupied private dwellings had no motor vehicle, 36.2% one, 36.3% two, 18.8% three or more (Census 2021, households) [official] — [ABS 2021 QuickStats](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/AUS)
- New Zealand: 6.3% of households had no motor vehicle, 33.5% one, 39.2% two (Census 2023; 8.6% of households gave no answer, so shares are of those answering) [official] — [Stats NZ, number of motor vehicles](https://datainfoplus.stats.govt.nz/item/nz.govt.stats/0605ad6d-d760-479f-a932-1c17c9e77b5f)
- Ireland: Census 2022 has a "households with cars" table, but the share was not in the results I saw — [CSO Census 2022 Table 15.1](https://census.geohive.ie/datasets/d0ec5b26b4a74f0ab1d903fd7c6d94ba_0/about)

### Inferences
- [calc] Car tile reach: US ~92%, England ~78%, Australia ~93%, NZ ~94% of households. Big cities (London, Dublin, NYC) will be much lower, which matters if early users are urban.

### Gaps
- Canada and Ireland household car shares: not found in this pass.
- Motorbike ownership: no good data found for any country. (Registrations exist in transport stats but I did not retrieve household shares.)
- 18 to 55 split: not found.

## My home: renting vs owning

### Takeaway
About two-thirds of households own and one-third rent in the UK (England), Canada, Australia, NZ and Ireland; the split of owners between "with mortgage" and "outright" is roughly even where known. Everyone lives somewhere, so the "My home" tile applies to nearly all adults; only the kind of reminders (rent vs mortgage vs repairs) changes.

### Cited Findings
- England 2023-24: owner occupiers 65% (35% outright, 30% with mortgage), private renters 19%, social renters 16% (households) [official] — [English Housing Survey 2023-24 headline report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6746f3242cdbaeed4c527f5f/Annex_A_-_2023-24_EHS_Headline_Report_on_household_demographics_and_resilience.pdf)
- Canada 2021: homeownership rate 66.5% (down from 69.0% in 2011); 33.1% of households rented [official, via summary] — [Statistics Canada housing](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/subjects-start/housing); [Globe and Mail on census](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-canada-homeownership-rate-drop/)
- Australia 2021: 31.0% owned outright, 35.0% owned with a mortgage, 30.6% rented (occupied private dwellings) [official] — [ABS 2021 QuickStats](https://www.abs.gov.au/census/find-census-data/quickstats/2021/AUS)
- New Zealand 2023: 66.0% of households own their home, up from 64.5% in 2018 [official] — [Stats NZ](https://www.stats.govt.nz/news/home-ownership-increases-and-housing-quality-improves/)
- Ireland 2022: owner-occupied share fell from almost 70% (2011) to 66%; private-renting households up 7% to 330,632 [official] — [CSO Census 2022 summary](https://www.cso.ie/en/csolatestnews/pressreleases/2023pressreleases/pressstatementcensusofpopulation2022-summaryresults/)

### Inferences
- Renters will be heavier among 18 to 35s everywhere, so for an app skewing young, "rent" reminders may matter more than "mortgage" ones.

### Gaps
- US tenure split (Census Housing Vacancy Survey, roughly mid-60s % owners) was not retrieved in this pass; do not use a number without checking.
- Canada mortgage vs outright split: one aggregator claimed "35.5% of homeowners have a mortgage", which looks wrong against other census reporting; left out as unreliable.
- 18 to 55 splits not found.

## Kids: households with dependent children under 18

### Takeaway
Only one figure retrieved: 39% of US *family* households include the householder's own children under 18 (2024). Share of *all* households will be lower (roughly a quarter, but that is not confirmed here).

### Cited Findings
- US 2024: about 39% of family households included the householder's children under 18; family households are nearly two-thirds of all households [official] — [US Census Bureau bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USCENSUS/bulletins/3c178b2); [Census "Nearly two-thirds are family households"](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USCENSUS/bulletins/3c16e18)

### Inferences
- [calc] 39% of ~two-thirds of households gives roughly 25% of all US households with own children under 18. This is a rough calculation, not an official figure.

### Gaps
- UK (ONS Families and Households), Canada, Australia, NZ, Ireland: not retrieved in this pass. These all exist in census outputs.
- Share of adults aged 18 to 55 who are parents of under-18s: no good data found.

## Parents: unpaid carers for parents or older relatives, sandwich generation

### Takeaway
Official censuses count about 6% (Ireland) to 9% (England and Wales) of people as unpaid carers; US and Canadian surveys with broader definitions find 25% to 42%. The tile will apply to a meaningful minority, and much more so for people in their 40s and 50s.

### Cited Findings
- US 2025: 63 million family caregivers, about 1 in 4 adults (59m caring for adults, 4m for children with illness or disability); average caregiver age 51; 29% of caregivers are "sandwich generation" (caring for children and adults) [survey, AARP and National Alliance for Caregiving] — [AARP Caregiving in the US 2025](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/ltss/family-caregiving/caregiving-in-the-us-2025/); [AARP press release](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers.html)
- England and Wales Census 2021: 5.0 million residents aged 5+ gave unpaid care, age-standardised 9.0% (England 8.9%, Wales 10.5%), down from 11.4% in 2011; women 10.4%, men 7.6% [official] — [ONS unpaid care, Census 2021](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/healthandsocialcare/healthandwellbeing/bulletins/unpaidcareenglandandwales/census2021)
- Carers UK argues the census undercounts; its own estimates are higher [survey/advocacy] — [Carers UK key facts](https://www.carersuk.org/policy-and-research/key-facts-and-figures/)
- Canada 2022: 13.4 million people 15+ (42%) gave unpaid care in the past year, but this includes caring for children under 15 as well as adults with long-term conditions [official] — [Statistics Canada, Providing care in Canada 2022](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-627-m/11-627-m2023004-eng.htm)
- Canada: StatCan has a dedicated study on people "sandwiched" between care for children and care-dependent adults (2024) — [StatCan sandwiched study](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/89-652-x/89-652-x2024002-eng.htm)
- Ireland Census 2022: 299,128 people gave regular unpaid care, up 53% from 195,263 in 2016; by county from 5% (Dublin City) to 7% (Mayo) [official] — [CSO Census 2022 Profile 4](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp4/censusofpopulation2022profile4-disabilityhealthandcarers/keyfindings/)

### Inferences
- [calc] Ireland: 299,128 out of a 2022 population of about 5.15 million is about 5.8%.
- Definitions differ a lot: censuses ask about regular care for someone with a health condition; AARP counts any help in the past year. For an app tile, the wider "helping ageing parents" group (lifts, appointments, bills) is probably bigger than any census figure.

### Gaps
- Share of carers whose care is specifically for a parent: not retrieved for any country.
- Regular financial support to parents: no good data found.
- Australia (Census 2021 "unpaid assistance to a person with a disability") and NZ figures: not retrieved.

## Pets

### Takeaway
Pets apply to roughly 6 in 10 to 7 in 10 households in Australia and NZ, and about half of UK adults own a dog or cat. All figures are industry surveys, not official statistics.

### Cited Findings
- Australia 2025: 73% of households have a pet (69% in 2022, 61% in 2019); dogs 49%, cats 34% [survey, Animal Medicines Australia] — [AMA Pets in Australia 2025](https://animalmedicinesaustralia.org.au/news-and-media/australias-most-comprehensive-pet-survey-shows-nearly-three-quarters-of-australian-homes-now-have-a-pet/)
- New Zealand 2024: 63% of households own a pet, steady for a decade; cats 40%, dogs 31% [survey, Companion Animals NZ] — [CANZ 2024 Pet Data Report](https://www.companionanimals.nz/2024-nz-pet-data-report)
- UK 2025: 30% of adults own a dog, 24% a cat, 2% a rabbit (survey of 10,975 adults 18+) [survey, PDSA] — [PDSA PAW mini report 2025](https://www.pdsa.org.uk/media/15783/pdsa-paw-mini-report-2025.pdf)
- Canada: "about 80% of households" have a pet, dogs 39%, cats 37% [weak, aggregator] — [Made in CA](https://madeinca.ca/pet-ownership-statistics-canada/); Canadian Animal Health Institute runs the underlying pet population survey — [CAHI](https://cahi-icsa.ca/canadian-pet-population-survey-highlights-the-importance-of-access-to-veterinary-care)
- Ireland: 25% of households owned a dog in 2023 [weak, Statista] — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/517020/households-owning-cats-dogs-europe-ireland/); an older Ipsos study for Pedigree and Whiskas reports 61% of households with a cat or dog (year unclear) [survey] — [Pet Ownership Study Ireland](https://dogadvocacyireland.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Pedigree-and-Whiskas-Pet-Ownership-Study-Findings.pdf)

### Inferences
- UK dog and cat overlap is unknown, so "any pet" for UK adults is somewhere around 45% to 55%; not a confirmed figure.

### Gaps
- US (APPA National Pet Owners Survey, usually quoted around two-thirds of households) not retrieved in this pass; check before using.
- The Canada 80% figure comes from an aggregator and looks high; treat as unconfirmed.

## Work or business: self-employed and people who file their own tax return

### Takeaway
Only partial data: the UK has about 11.5 million Self Assessment filers (roughly 1 in 5 adults), and about 16% of Australian workers are self-employed (ILO modelled estimate).

### Cited Findings
- UK: 11,489,825 Self Assessment returns received by 31 January 2026 for the 2024-25 tax year, 97.25% online; more than 12 million expected for 2025-26 [official, HMRC] — [HMRC press release](https://hm-revenue-customs-hmrc.mynewsdesk.com/pressreleases/11-dot-48-million-beat-the-self-assessment-deadline-3430357); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/298905-self-assessment-filers-quick-off-the-mark)
- Australia: self-employed 16.1% of total employment (Dec 2025, down from 16.2% in Dec 2024; ILO modelled estimate) [official-modelled, via aggregator] — [CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/australia/employment-and-unemployment/au-selfemployed-modeled-ilo-estimate--of-total-employment)
- UK: growth in solo self-employment has been among the highest in the OECD [research] — [IFS](https://ifs.org.uk/publications/what-does-rise-self-employment-tell-us-about-uk-labour-market)

### Inferences
- [calc] UK: 11.5 million filers against roughly 54 million adults is about 1 in 5 adults who must file their own return. Filers include landlords, higher earners and pensioners, not just the self-employed. The adult population figure is from general knowledge, not a source retrieved here.

### Gaps
- US self-employment share (BLS) and share of adults who file a federal return (IRS): not retrieved.
- UK self-employed count (ONS Labour Force Survey, usually cited around 4.2 to 4.4 million): not confirmed in this pass.
- Canada, NZ, Ireland self-employment shares; Australian individual tax returns lodged (ATO Taxation Statistics): not retrieved.

## Travel: passport holders and travel abroad

### Takeaway
Passport holding ranges from about half of Americans to about 85% of people in England and Wales. The Travel tile is relevant to most adults in the UK and Canada and about half in the US and Australia.

### Cited Findings
- England and Wales Census 2021: 84.4% of usual residents held one passport and 2.1% held more than one; 13.2% in England held none. The census counts expired passports people could renew [official] — [ONS TS005 Passports held](https://www.ons.gov.uk/datasets/TS005/editions/2021); [ONS dual citizens article](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/internationalmigration/articles/dualcitizenslivinginenglandandwales/census2021)
- Wales 2021: 81.2% held at least one passport, 77.2% a UK passport [official] — [Welsh Government](https://www.gov.wales/demography-and-migration-wales-census-2021-html)
- Australia 2024-25: over 15 million Australians hold a current passport, over 56% of the population [official, Australian Passport Office] — [Passport facts 2024-25](https://www.passports.gov.au/passport-facts-2024-25)
- US 2024: about 51% of residents hold a valid passport (based on State Department circulation counts) [weak, secondary calculation] — [Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/us-passport-increase)
- Canada: government estimate of about 70% holding a valid passport [weak, via search summary; no primary source retrieved] — [search result page](https://www.threads.com/@late.july/post/DFX7MCFJHDb) (low quality: do not rely on it)

### Inferences
- The UK census figure (includes expired) will run higher than "currently valid" counts used for the US and Australia, so these are not strictly comparable.

### Gaps
- Share travelling abroad each year: no good data found for any country in this pass (sources to check: ONS Travel Trends, ABS overseas arrivals and departures, US NTTO outbound, StatCan travel survey, CSO household travel).
- NZ and Ireland passport holding: no good data found.

## Health: chronic conditions, routine check-ups, dental visits

### Takeaway
Only the US dental figure was retrieved: two-thirds of US adults had a dental visit in the past year. Chronic condition shares were not found in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- US: 66.7% of adults 18+ had a dental exam or cleaning in the past year (NHIS 2025 early release) [official, CDC] — [CDC FastStats oral and dental health](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/fastats/dental.htm)
- US: CDC has a separate figure for adults 18 to 64 with a dental visit, by urban vs rural [official] — [CDC QuickStats 18 to 64](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/71/wr/mm7116a3.htm?s_cid=mm7116a3_w)

### Inferences
- Health reminders (dentist, check-ups, repeat medicine) apply broadly; the dental figure shows about a third of US adults do not go yearly, which is a gentle-reminder opportunity.

### Gaps
- Chronic condition prevalence (CDC, ABS National Health Survey, AIHW, NZ Health Survey, CSO Healthy Ireland, StatCan CCHS, ONS/NHS): not retrieved.
- Annual physical rates: no good data found.
- Dental visits outside the US: not retrieved.

## Money: subscriptions per adult

### Takeaway
No official figures exist; commercial surveys vary widely (about 3 to 8 paid subscriptions per US adult depending on what is counted). Money tasks (bills, tax, subscriptions) apply to essentially every adult.

### Cited Findings
- US: "3.4 active paid subscriptions" per consumer (2026) and "8.2" when music, gaming, fitness and software are counted (2025) [weak, aggregator compiling industry reports] — [Self.inc](https://www.self.inc/info/cost-of-unused-paid-subscriptions/)
- US: average of 4 paid streaming subscriptions (Deloitte 2025, as cited) [weak, secondary] — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/research/state-of-streaming/)
- UK: 88% of consumers have at least one subscription; those with one spend £50.60 a month on average [survey, Barclays] — [Barclays subscription economy 2025](https://home.barclays/insights/2025/10/Next-Phase-Subscription-Economy/)
- UK: 2.8 subscriptions per person, £65.50 a month (Aqua 2025, as cited) [weak, secondary] — [Fortunly](https://fortunly.com/statistics/subscription-spending-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Any adult who pays bills fits the Money tile; subscription counts only matter for how many reminders the tile seeds.

### Gaps
- Canada, Australia, NZ, Ireland subscription counts: no good data found.
