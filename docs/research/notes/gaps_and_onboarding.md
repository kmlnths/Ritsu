# Gaps in the 10 life tiles, and how other apps onboard

Researched 2026-09-30. About 20 searches or fetches. Several key pages (PMC, Medium, Mobbin) blocked the fetch, so some findings rest on search snippets and are marked that way. Anything with no source is in Gaps or Inferences, never in Cited Findings.

## Q1. Which recurring life responsibilities do the 10 tiles miss, and how common is each?

### Takeaway
Faith practice is the biggest gap with good evidence. Daily prayer is a majority habit in India (60 to 71%) and the Philippines (79%), common in the US (44%), and a minority habit in the UK (21%). Chronic conditions that need regular checks are very common in India (hypertension 35.5%, diabetes 11.4% of adults). Period tracking is used by about 6 in 10 women in the studies found. In Singapore, a home with a live-in domestic worker is ordinary (301,600 workers). The other gaps (birthdays, festivals, plants, gym, digital chores, document expiry, relationships, therapy, volunteering) are plausible, but I found no country-level numbers on them in this pass.

### Cited Findings
**Faith and religious observance**
- Daily prayer, Pew 2025 36-country survey: India 71%, Philippines 79%, US 44%, UK 21%. Indonesia is highest at 95%. — [Pew, 2025-05-06](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/06/spiritual-and-religious-practices/); summary via [Visual Capitalist](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/visualized-which-countries-pray-the-most/)
- Pew's US comparison says the US is "roughly in the middle" of the 35 other countries on daily prayer, but stands out among rich countries. — [Pew, 2025-06-25](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/25/spirituality-and-religion-us-comparison-to-other-countries/)
- India 2021 (a separate Pew survey of about 30,000 adults): 60% pray daily (Christians 77%, Jains 73%, Sikhs 45%, Buddhists 38%). 71% visit a place of worship monthly or more. 81% have a home altar or shrine. **Conflict:** this 60% differs from the 71% in the 2025 survey. The surveys, years and wording differ, so give a range rather than one figure. — [Pew India, 2021](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/06/29/religious-practices-2/)
- I found no Pew fasting figures (Ramadan, Ekadashi, Lent) in the fetched section. See Gaps.

**Chronic conditions that need regular checks**
- India, ICMR-INDIAB (113,043 people, all states, published in The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology in 2023): diabetes 11.4% (about 101 million people), prediabetes 15.3%, hypertension 35.5% (about 315 million). By state, diabetes ranges from 4.8% (Uttar Pradesh) to 26.4% (Goa), and hypertension from 24.3% to 51.8% (Punjab). — [Lancet D&E 2023](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(23)00119-5/fulltext); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37301218/)
- Thyroid, and all non-India countries, were not researched in this pass. See Gaps.

**Women's health**
- A systematic review and meta-analysis (studies from 2017 to 2025) puts the pooled share of women using menstruation-tracking apps at 61% (95% CI 45 to 75%), with single studies ranging from 37% to 85%. The main uses are cycle monitoring, planning or avoiding pregnancy, and symptoms. — [PMC systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13477691/) (from the search snippet; not fetched)
- 2024 survey of 700 US millennial and Gen Z women: 62.3% used period trackers, mainly to predict their next period. — [JMIR 2024](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e53146)

**Domestic workers**
- Singapore: 301,600 migrant domestic workers were employed in December 2024. — [MOM foreign workforce numbers](https://www.mom.gov.sg/foreign-workforce-numbers)
- Philippines: about 624,000 domestic workers in the country (figure from an older ILO source; year unclear). Filipinos are also a main source of domestic workers for Hong Kong and Singapore. — [ILO Southeast Asia brief](https://www.ilo.org/media/321431/download)
- India: I found no reliable 2024 count. ILO notes the numbers are hard to measure. — [ILO global and regional statistics](https://www.ilo.org/media/458491/download)

### Inferences
- **Near universal:** birthdays and anniversaries, festivals and gifts, and digital chores such as backups and passwords (all unmeasured here, but they apply to anyone with family and a phone). Document expiry also applies to almost every adult (driving licence, passport, and in India, Aadhaar/PAN updates), but it happens only every few years.
- **Group or life-stage specific:** faith (strong in IN, PH, MY and Muslim-majority groups; weak in UK, AU, NZ); chronic monitoring (rises with age; very common in India); women's health (about half of users, and life-stage dependent: periods, then pregnancy, then screening); domestic workers (SG, MY, HK, and Indian middle-class homes; almost none in UK, AU, NZ); plants and garden, gym memberships, therapy, volunteering (hobby or subgroup).
- **Where the gaps fit in the current tiles:**
  - Chronic monitoring and women's health fit inside **Health** as optional sub-picks, not new tiles.
  - Domestic worker salary and leave fit inside **My home** as a regional sub-pick (shown for SG, MY, PH, IN).
  - Birthdays and festivals fit a **People** idea that could merge with **Parents**. Candidate: rename "Parents" to "Family and friends" so it covers parents, birthdays, anniversaries, calling friends and gifts.
  - Document expiry fits **Money** or a new "Papers" item; it fits as a sub-item without a new tile.
- **Faith is the one gap strong enough, and distinct enough, to justify its own tile** in IN, PH, MY, SG and US markets. It must stay neutral (no default religion), and prayers must not count toward the day in a way that feels like judging.
- Ritsu rule check: chronic monitoring means reminders to check or record only, never target numbers or advice ("ask your doctor for yours"), which matches the existing health-gap rule.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced share of people who fast (Ramadan, Ekadashi, Lent) by country. Pew's other reports probably have it; not fetched.
- Diabetes and hypertension prevalence for US, UK, CA, AU, NZ, SG, PH and MY was not researched. IDF Diabetes Atlas and WHO hypertension reports are the next places to look. Thyroid prevalence was not researched.
- Screening uptake (cervical, breast) by country was not researched.
- India and Malaysia domestic worker counts were not found.
- I found no survey data on how common these are as regular chores: birthdays, festival gifting, plants, gym membership renewals, digital housekeeping, document expiry, relationship rituals, therapy, volunteering. Do not quote numbers for these.

## Q2. How do comparable apps ask what a person's life includes during onboarding?

### Takeaway
Wellness apps mostly use a short goal quiz (Headspace, Fabulous; Noom takes this to an extreme). Task and chore apps mostly start empty or pick by room (Tody, Sweepy) and let the user add things over time. Bill and medicine apps (Paytm, Medisafe) set up reminders one item at a time, when the user adds a real item, not during a first-run survey. The best controlled evidence (Headspace) shows a short question step raises first-session action even when the answer changes nothing, but it did not raise long-term use.

### Cited Findings
- **Headspace experiment** (Irrational Labs with Purchasely; 5 arms): course starts rose from 31% (control) to 63% (the "perceived fit" arm: a short personalisation quiz followed by the same default Basics course). A plan-making ("precommitment") arm raised app opens by 7.5% and unique return days by 4%. **No arm significantly raised active meditation days.** Sample size and quiz questions were not published on the page. — [Purchasely](https://www.purchasely.com/blog/headspace-behavioral-science-onboarding-experiment)
- **Noom:** a web-to-app funnel of up to 113 screens taking 10 to 15 minutes, with branching (for example, extra questions if you say you have diabetes). — [RevenueCat teardown](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel)
- **Fabulous** asks about 11 heavy questions on life and goals up front. **Finch** asks almost no goal questions and puts the pet character first. — [Bootcamp/Medium](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/main-character-energy-how-two-habit-building-apps-build-motivation-in-onboarding-a3d144bd2818) (snippet only; page returned 403)
- **Headspace** asks why you came (goal selection) and builds meditation into your routine. — [Appcues GoodUX](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/headspaces-mindful-onboarding-sequence) (snippet only)
- **Tody and Sweepy** organise chores by room. The user picks rooms, then accepts suggested chores or adds their own. — [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/tag/4-android-apps-taking-care-household-chores/)
- **Todoist** onboards through a "Getting started" template project, plus a large public template library the user can import later. — [Todoist templates](https://www.todoist.com/templates/todoist-onboarding); [Todoist help](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/introduction-to-templates-in-todoist-uofJ8i40M)
- **Paytm** bill reminders are set per bill after fetching it (pick the provider, enter the consumer number, tap Fetch Bill, turn on the reminder), with some billers offering "3 days before". — [Paytm support](https://paytm.com/support/bill-payment/setting-up-reminders-for-pending-bill-payments-on-paytm)
- **Medisafe** sets up medicines in a guided flow for each therapy (dosing windows, titration), and its reminders adapt to the person's routine. — [Medisafe](https://medisafe.com/solutions/for-patients)

### Inferences
- Ritsu's "tap tiles now, ask for dates later, one a day" is a hybrid: pick by area like Tody's rooms, then fill details over time like Paytm and Medisafe. None of the verified apps asks life-admin dates up front, which supports the plan.
- The Headspace result suggests the tile screen is valuable even if some tiles only change which templates are shown. The act of choosing raises commitment. Do not expect it alone to raise long-term use.

### Gaps
- Not verified this pass: TickTick, Any.do, Structured, Tiimo, Habitica, Apple Health, Google Fit, Calm, Flo, CRED, Practo, 1mg, Life Admin, Centriq, HomeZada. From memory only (unverified; treat as leads): Flo asks your goal (track periods, get pregnant, pregnancy), and Tiimo and Structured ask about routines and wake time. Check against Page Flows or Mobbin screenshots before citing.
- The Mobbin "1,460 onboarding flows" study could not be fetched (403). It likely has median screen counts by category.

## Q3. What does the evidence say about onboarding length, number of choices on one screen, and progressive onboarding?

### Takeaway
"Fewer options is always better" is not supported as a general law. The 2010 meta-analysis found an average choice-overload effect of about zero, but with large variance. The 2015 meta-analysis shows overload appears when options are complex, the person is unsure what they want, the task is hard, or the goal is to minimise effort. First-run setup hits at least two of these (the person is unsure, and wants low effort). So keep tiles simple, familiar and skippable, and learn the rest over time. NN/g's evidence favours help at the moment of use over front-loaded tutorials.

### Cited Findings
- Iyengar and Lepper (2000) jam study: 24 jams drew 60% of passers-by against 40% for 6 jams, but 3% of tasters bought from 24 against 30% from 6. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/12189991_When_Choice_is_Demotivating_Can_One_Desire_Too_Much_of_a_Good_Thing); summarised by [digitalwellbeing.org](https://digitalwellbeing.org/the-jam-study-strikes-back-when-less-choice-does-mean-more-sales/)
- Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd 2010 meta-analysis (63 conditions, 50 experiments, N = 5,036): the mean effect is "virtually zero", with considerable variance, and no sufficient conditions for overload were found. — [JCR 2010](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/37/3/409/1827647)
- Chernev, Böckenholt and Goodman 2015 meta-analysis (99 observations, N = 7,202): overload is reliably driven by four factors, namely set complexity, task difficulty, preference uncertainty, and an effort-minimising goal. — [J Consumer Psych 2015 PDF](https://chernev.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/ChoiceOverload_JCP_2015.pdf)
- NN/g (Laubheimer, 2023-02-12): tutorials "don't result in better task performance" and are often skipped. Help shown out of context is hard to remember. Contextual "pull" help works better. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/)

### Inferences
- 10 tiles is within reason if each is a plain, familiar life word with an icon, multi-select is allowed, and "skip" is fine (low complexity, low difficulty). Going to 12 or 14 would raise complexity. Adding sub-questions inside tiles at setup would raise difficulty.
- Suggested ordering: the most common tiles first (Health, My home, Money, Work or studies), then the group-specific ones (Kids, Pets, Car or bike, Faith, Travel).
- The "one question a day on Home" plan is progressive profiling, which is consistent with NN/g's findings in favour of contextual help.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous public study on drop-off per extra onboarding screen. Figures like "X% drop per step" are vendor blog claims and were not cited.
- I found no study testing Hick's law on multi-select tile screens specifically. Hick's law is about reaction time for a single choice, so it applies only loosely here.

## Q4. What is the evidence on reminder fatigue in habit and health apps?

### Takeaway
Reminders give a short-term lift that weakens over time, too many make people turn notifications off or delete the app, and depending on reminders can weaken the habit itself. This supports Ritsu's calm, one-a-day rule and not adding a reminder stream for every tile.

### Cited Findings
- A micro-randomised trial of push notifications in an mHealth app (Bidargaddi et al., JMIR mHealth 2018) tested whether prompts raise engagement in the next short window. — [PMC6293241](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6293241/) (fetch blocked by a captcha; I cannot confirm effect sizes here)
- Push notifications work as short-term cues, but consistency in a language-learning app fell sharply once reminders stopped, suggesting reliance on outside cues. Tapering reminders is advised. — [ResearchGate 2025/26 preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401241990_Push_Notifications_and_Habit_Formation_Behavioral_Impact_on_Daily_Language_Practice_Consistency) (search snippet; preprint quality unknown)
- Contextual, personalised notifications raised perceived usefulness; overly frequent alerts caused fatigue. — [ResearchGate, mHealth weight maintenance](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380521578_Investigating_Contextual_Notifications_to_Drive_Self-Monitoring_in_mHealth_Apps_for_Weight_Maintenance) (snippet)
- How notifications affect engagement in a health app (JMIR mHealth 2023). — [JMIR mHealth 2023](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2023/1/e38342) (not fetched; cite only as further reading)

### Inferences
- Each tile should add few default reminders (for example, 2 to 4 things that come back, mostly monthly or yearly), not a daily stream. Daily items (prayers, medicine) should be opt-in inside the tile.
- Faith and chronic-condition tiles are the ones most likely to create daily notifications, so they need the strictest defaults.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm effect sizes from the micro-randomised trial because the fetch was blocked.
- I found no strong data on the share of users who turn off notifications in habit apps specifically. Vendor claims were not cited.

---

### Recommendation (opinion, based on the above)
- **Add one tile: Faith** (neutral wording such as "Faith and prayer"). It has strong evidence of being common in 5 of the 10 target countries, and it does not fit any existing tile.
- **Merge or rename:** "Parents" becomes "Family" (parents, birthdays, anniversaries, festivals and gifts, calling people). "Studies" and "Work or business" could merge into "Work or studies" to keep the count at 10.
- **Keep the gaps as sub-picks, not tiles:** chronic checks and women's health go under Health; domestic worker salary and leave go under My home (shown only in SG, MY, PH, IN); document expiry goes under Money; plants go under My home; gym goes under Health.
- **Drop nothing else.** Plants, therapy, volunteering and digital chores can live in the Templates library (the user adds them later), not on the setup screen.
